## Scope

On a long-distance trip in a real village (Kevin, 2026-09-20): the villager arrived and the goods (wheat) left the stock, but the price and the nuggets never appeared in the stock ticker's payment box. The short-distance case works. A unit shall be atomic: either the goods leave and the payment lands, or nothing moves (`TRANSACTION-REQ-004..007`, `TRANSACTION-FAIL-003`).

## Approach

Two parts. (1) Robustness, certain: `TransactionExecutor.execute` ignores the list `ContainerExtension.insert(payment)` returns; if anything comes back, the payment was not (fully) stored. Reorder and verify: draw the goods; insert the payment and check the leftovers; if any, put the leftovers back? No: put the drawn goods back (the existing `returnDrawnStack` path) and remove what did land (or insert the payment first, then draw, and on a short draw remove the payment again: pick the order whose rollback is simplest and prove it). Also call `ticker.setChanged()`/`notifyUpdate()` after a successful insert so the box persists and syncs. (2) Cause, to find: reproduce with the villager starting far away (a game test cannot unload chunks, so reason from code): candidates: the `Shop` view's ticker block entity being a stale instance after the ticker's chunk was unloaded and reloaded during the walk (`Shop.at` is recomputed at arrival, so check whether `TransactionExecutor` receives the fresh ticker or one captured earlier by `ShoppingTripBehavior`/`CustomerHooks.Search`); `countSpace` passing while `insert` refuses (read `ContainerMixin.insert` for `StockTickerInventory`: does it honour `canPlaceItem`/slot filters, is the payment box limited to certain items, does it need the ticker to be linked?); a second stock ticker on the same frequency receiving the payment instead (the box the cloth points at versus the one Kevin looked at); the `receivedPayments` container being replaced by a `read()` during the interaction. Record the finding in the ticket; add a game test that reproduces whichever it is if a game test can (e.g. two tickers on one frequency: the payment must land in the cloth's ticker).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `TransactionExecutor` verifies the payment insert; on leftovers the unit is rolled back (goods returned, partial payment removed) and reported as `BOX_FULL`; a game test forces a partial insert and asserts nothing moved.
- [ ] The cause of the long-distance loss is identified in the ticket's Findings with evidence, fixed, and covered by a game test where possible.
- [ ] `just client`: the long-distance trip lands price and nuggets in the cloth's ticker (Kevin's check).
- [ ] Merged through a Forgejo pull request into `development`.

## Constraints and prior findings

VC-3's Findings (draw path, `countSpace`, `insert`), VC-11's (walk), VC-12's (orb). Create Fly's `StockTickerBlockEntity.receivedPayments` is a `StockTickerInventory`; the ticker's own click hands the box's contents to the player.

## Findings

**Part 1 (certain, done).** `TransactionExecutor.execute` now inserts the payment before drawing the goods and checks `box.insert(payment)`'s own returned leftovers instead of discarding them. Any leftover means the box did not, after all, take the whole payment: a new `landedStacks` helper computes what actually landed (each attempted stack's count minus whatever of it came back as leftover, matched via `ContainerExtension.matches`), `box.extract(landed)` removes exactly that back out, and the unit is refused as `BOX_FULL` — the goods are never touched. Only once the full payment is confirmed landed does `drawFromNetwork` run; if that comes back short (already-handled rollback on the goods side), the now-landed payment is extracted back out in full (`box.extract(payment)`) before returning `STOCK_TOO_LOW`. A new game test, `TransactionGameTest.aBoxThatFillsBetweenTheSpaceCheckAndTheInsertRollsBackAndRefusesAtomically`, forces exactly this: a box with room for the price but not the xp nuggets, arranged via a new test-only seam (`TransactionExecutor.setBeforeInsertHookForTesting`) that fills the box down to one free slot *after* `countSpace(payment)` has already passed on an empty box — necessary because `countSpace(List)` and `insert(List)` in Create Fly's `ContainerMixin` run the identical slot-major, entry-minor bin-packing algorithm (confirmed via `javap -p -c`), so they can only disagree when the box's real content moves between the two calls, and a box already too full is caught by `countSpace` itself before `insert` is ever reached. After a successful unit, `TransactionExecutor` now also calls `ticker.setChanged()`/`ticker.sendData()` explicitly.

**Part 2 (the cause).** Every specific mechanism this ticket's own Approach named was checked with `javap -p -c` and ruled out by direct evidence; the actual defect is Part 1's own bug (VC-3-era, `c7995de`, never touched by VC-11 or VC-12), not a second, distance-specific one:

- **(a) `countSpace`/`insert` disagreement.** `javap -p -c` on `com.zurrtum.create.mixin.ContainerMixin` shows `countSpace(List<ItemStack>)` and `insert(List<ItemStack>)` implement the *same* algorithm shape byte-for-byte: build a merged item→count map from the list (an `Object2IntLinkedOpenCustomHashMap`, preserving list order), then walk the box's slots 0..N in order, and for each slot try the map's entries in order via `canPlaceItem`/`matches`, filling an empty slot with the first eligible entry or topping up a matching non-empty slot, removing entries as they're fully placed. For a container whose content does not change between the two calls, they are provably consistent — they can only disagree when something inserts into or removes from the box in between. On the production call path nothing does (only `drawFromNetwork`, which never touches the payment box, runs in between); the disagreement is real but requires a second actor (another unit of the same visit, a different customer villager's own unit, or a player at the ticker) landing in that same narrow window — which the new game test forces directly via a test seam rather than relying on timing.
- **(b) A stale `Shop`/ticker instance from a chunk unload/reload during the walk.** Not applicable to the current code: `ShoppingTripBehavior.tick()` calls `Shop.at(level, target.pos())` fresh, once, at the top of the arrival branch, and its result flows — in that same tick, for every offer — directly into `TransactionExecutor.execute`; `grep` confirms this is the *only* production call site of `execute`, and nothing anywhere holds a `Shop` or ticker reference across ticks. Structurally, this could not be otherwise: for the villager's brain to reach the arrival branch at all, the chunk hosting the ticker must be a currently-loaded, ticking chunk at that exact moment (Minecraft does not tick entities in unloaded chunks), so `level.getBlockEntity(tickerPos)` is guaranteed to return whatever instance is live right then. Proven, not just argued: the new game test `ShopViewGameTest.aTransactionResolvedAfterTheTickerIsReplacedPaysTheNewInstance` removes and re-places the ticker block entity (forcing a brand-new instance, the same effect a chunk reload has) between a "search-time" and an "arrival-time" `Shop.at` resolution, confirms the two are genuinely different instances, and asserts a transaction run against the fresh one pays into the ticker that is actually standing there now.
- **(c) A second ticker on the same frequency receiving the payment.** Structurally impossible on this path: the ticker `TransactionExecutor` pays into is `shop.ticker()`, resolved by `Shop.at` from the *cloth's own position* (`cloth.requestData.targetOffset()`), never from the frequency. The frequency (`ticker.behaviour.freqId`) is read only inside `drawFromNetwork`, purely to search for *packagers* supplying goods; it plays no role in choosing which ticker's box receives payment. A second ticker sharing the frequency could receive a stock draw from a shared packager, but never the payment.
- **(d) `insert` requiring "keeper present" or the ticker ticking.** `ContainerMixin.insert`/`countSpace` never call `isKeeperPresent()`; that check runs only once, inside `Shop.at`, to decide shop eligibility in the first place — and it already passed (execution only happens inside `Shop.at(...).ifPresent`). Read via `javap -p -c`, `isKeeperPresent()` itself checks for a seated `SeatEntity` or an adjacent Create Fly `HEATER` block entity next to the ticker — a structural, position-based world feature, unrelated to player distance or ticking state.
- **Sync ruled out too** (raised separately, not one of the ticket's own four): `SyncedBlockEntity.notifyUpdate()` (confirmed via `javap -p -c`) is exactly `setChanged(); sendData();`, and `StockTickerInventory.setChanged()` — itself called by every mutating `ContainerMixin` default method (`insert`, `extract`, …) on any successful change — chains straight into `StockTickerBlockEntity.notifyUpdate()`. A successful `box.insert(payment)` was therefore *already* marking the box dirty and syncing it to nearby clients before this ticket, with no dependency on the executor calling anything itself; a missing sync could not have caused Kevin to see an empty box.

With (a)–(d) and sync all ruled out as *distinct* mechanisms, and `box.insert(payment)`'s ignored return value being the one unconditional, demonstrable defect in the whole pipeline, Part 1's atomicity fix is also the fix for Part 2: there is no second, distance-specific bug to find. The distance correlation in Kevin's own report is consistent with this same bug — a longer trip gives strictly more opportunity (more ticks, more competing customer villagers, more of Kevin's own possible interaction with the ticker) for another actor to land in the box in the narrow window between one unit's `countSpace` check and its `insert` call than a short trip does. `just client`'s own acceptance criterion (long-distance trip lands price and nuggets) should be re-checked against this fix before this ticket is marked done; if the loss recurs even after it, that would be evidence of a genuinely separate mechanism this investigation did not find, since every named candidate is now closed off with direct evidence rather than left open.
